That Work

Your slides are not your notes. Twenty exercises on titles, figures, text and what to cut.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Your slides are not your notes. If everything you plan to say is on the screen, the audience reads faster than you speak — and then stops listening to you entirely.

A script"Results: The output of the system decreased by 12% over the twelve-week test period, which we believe is due to progressive fouling of the heat exchanger surface…"Nobody listens to you while they read this. And the title says nothing.
A slide"Output fell 12% over twelve weeks" + the graphThe title is the finding. The graph is the evidence. You supply the reasoning out loud.

Six rules

RuleWhy
One idea per slideIf you need "and" in the title, it is two slides.
Title states the finding"Output fell 12%" beats "Results". A reader who sees only your titles should get your argument.
Text is a cue, not a scriptRoughly six words per line. You say the sentences.
24 pt minimumIf it does not read from the back of the room, it is not on the slide.
Label every axisQuantity and unit. A graph nobody can decode is decoration.
Never read the slide aloudThe audience is already ahead of you.

Figures on slides are not figures in reports

A report figure can be dense — the reader controls the pace. A slide figure gets about fifteen seconds. Strip anything that is not part of the point: extra series, gridlines, second axes, decimal places nobody needs.

Say the axes before the pattern. "The horizontal axis is time over twelve weeks; the vertical is output in kilowatt hours. You can see the fall from week three." Reverse that order and the audience is still decoding while you are explaining.

Colour

  • Do not rely on colour alone to separate lines — about one in twelve men has some colour vision deficiency. Use different line styles or direct labels as well.
  • Dark text on a light background survives bad projectors better than the reverse.
  • Two or three colours is usually enough for a technical talk.

What to cut

Animations that make the audience wait, transitions between slides, clip art, your university logo on every slide, and slide numbers so small nobody can quote them in questions. A number in the corner at readable size is genuinely useful in Q&A.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
In this slide we can see the results.This slide shows the results.
As we can observe in the graphic…As you can see in the graph…
I will show you a scheme of the system.I will show you a diagram of the system.
In the next slide I explain the method.On the next slide I will explain the method.
Three false friends on one slide. Gráfico is a graph or chart, not a "graphic". Esquema is a diagram, not a "scheme". And you speak on a slide, not in it.
