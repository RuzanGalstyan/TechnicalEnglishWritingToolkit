Presenting
as a Team

Your project defence is a team presentation. Twenty exercises on handovers, question routing, and looking like one team.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Your project defence is a team presentation. Examiners notice a team that behaves like one team, and they notice a team that behaves like four separate talks stapled together.

Four talks"…and that is my part. Ana?"No handover, no link. The audience has to work out that a new section has started.
One team"That covers the method. Ana will now take you through what we found."Closes your section, names the next speaker, and says what is coming.

Handing over

A handover does three things: it closes your part, names the next speaker, and says what they will cover.

WeakBetter
Ana?That covers the method. Ana will now take you through the results.
Now my colleague speaks.I will hand over to Ana, who will explain the test data.
Next slide please.Having described the setup, we can turn to what it produced. Ana.

Before the day

  • Agree the order and the boundaries. Who covers what, and where each part stops.
  • Agree the timings and rehearse against a clock. Teams overrun far more often than individuals.
  • Use one slide template. Four visual styles tell the examiner you never met.
  • Decide who fields which questions — and name one person to route them.
  • Agree what you disagree about. Contradicting each other in Q&A is the single most damaging thing a team can do.

While your team-mates speak

You are still on stage. Look at the speaker or the screen, not at your phone or your notes. An audience reads a bored team as a team that does not believe its own work.

Routing a questionThat is really Ana's area — Ana, would you take that?
Adding to a colleagueTo add briefly to what Ana said, …
You disagree with a colleagueWe looked at that both ways. Ana's reading is X; I would put more weight on Y.
Nobody knowsWe do not have data on that, but we would expect …

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
I pass the word to Ana. (Paso la palabra…)I will hand over to Ana.
Ana will explain us the results.Ana will explain the results to us.
In first place, we will present…First, we will present…
My partner Ana will continue. (compañera)My colleague Ana will continue.
Careful with partner. In English it usually suggests a romantic or business partner. For a classmate or team-mate, say colleague, team-mate or the rest of my group.

Sources
