AI ethics in writing

When Is AI Use
Acceptable?

Twenty coursework situations with no obvious answer, and how to write a disclosure that reads as competence.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Most institutions now expect you to say what you used. Students write one vague sentence and it satisfies nobody — a specific disclosure reads as competence, not as a confession.

Tells a marker nothing"AI was used in the preparation of this report."Used for what? At which stage? On whose text?
Reads as competence"I used an AI assistant to check grammar in a draft I had written myself. All content, structure, sources and analysis are my own. I reviewed each suggested change individually."What, when, what remains yours, what you checked.

Three questions that settle most cases

1. What is being assessed? If the assignment assesses your writing in English, having a tool write it defeats the assessment. If it assesses engineering judgement and the writing is incidental, the calculation differs. Read the learning outcomes on the brief.
2. Could you defend every sentence? If your supervisor stopped you in the corridor and asked why a claim is in your report, could you answer? This catches problems no policy anticipates.
3. Would you be comfortable if it were listed? Imagine a line in your methods describing exactly what you used. If writing that sentence feels uncomfortable, the discomfort is the finding.

Two things that are not integrity questions

Confidentiality. On placement or with company data, pasting into a public tool may breach an NDA or data-protection law regardless of your academic policy.

Accuracy. These tools produce fluent text that can be wrong, and invent citations that look entirely plausible. Anything you keep, you own.

Writing a disclosure

A good disclosure names four things:

  • What you used it for — the specific task, not the category
  • At what stage — before drafting, on your own draft, after completion
  • What remains yours — content, structure, sources, analysis
  • What you checked — particularly facts and references
Where it goes varies. Cover page, methods section, appendix, or a form — check your brief. If nothing is specified, a short paragraph before the reference list is a reasonable default, but ask.
