Why this matters

When you cite something you are telling your reader: I checked this, and it holds up. Your name goes on the work, so the credibility of your sources becomes your credibility. One weak source in a strong report is what a marker circles.

Five checks

Run them in this order. If a source fails badly on one, that is usually enough — but note which, because that is what you would say if challenged.

CheckAsk
1 CurrencyHas anything happened since this was published that would change it?
2 RelevanceWould removing this source weaken my argument?
3 AuthorityWho would be accountable if this turned out to be wrong?
4 AccuracyCould I trace this claim back to its original evidence?
5 PurposeWho benefits if I believe this?
The working question is not “is this true?” — you often cannot tell. It is “would I be comfortable defending this source if my marker asked why I used it?” That one you can always answer.

Where students go wrong

  • Stopping at the first result. Search ranking measures popularity and optimisation, not reliability.
  • Trusting the surface. Design is cheap.
  • Confusing “cited a lot” with “correct”. Retracted papers keep collecting citations for years.
  • Ignoring the date in a field that has moved on.

Two modern traps

Predatory journals. Some publishers charge a fee and publish almost anything with minimal review. Warning signs: promises of publication within days, a scope covering every field at once, an editorial board you cannot verify, and persistent email invitations. If you have not heard of the journal, check whether your library indexes it.

Generated content. Text that reads fluently and cites papers that do not exist. If a reference looks perfect but you cannot open it, that is your finding.

No source is perfect

The goal is not a flawless source but knowing the weaknesses of the one you chose — and saying so. “Although based on a small sample, Chen (2024) suggests…” is stronger writing than pretending the limitation is not there.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
This web page is very actual.This website is very recent / up to date.
The article is from a prestigious revist.The article is from a respected journal.
The source is fiable.The source is reliable.
The editorial published it in 2019.The publisher published it in 2019.
Three false friends in one sentence. Revista is a journal (academic) or magazine (popular) — never “revist”. Editorial is a publisher; an English editorial is an opinion piece. And actual means current.
