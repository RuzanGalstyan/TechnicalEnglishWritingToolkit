How Certain Should You Sound?

Say something too strongly and one counter-example destroys it. Say it too weakly and it means nothing. Twenty-five exercises to find the right level.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

In your report you will write sentences like the coating reduces wear. How certain you sound is a choice, and your marker notices it.

Too strong"This proves the coating reduces wear."
Five samples. One failure destroys the claim.
Too weak"It could possibly be suggested that there may perhaps be an effect."
Four hedges. It claims nothing, so it earns nothing.
Right"These results suggest that the coating reduces wear."
Honest about the evidence, and still a real finding.

This decision appears in every results section, every discussion, and every literature review.

The five ways to soften a claim

TypeWordsWhat it does
Modal verbmay, might, couldSays it is possible, not certain
Hedging verbsuggest, indicate, appear, seem, tend toReports evidence without claiming proof
Adverb or adjectivegenerally, probably, slightly, partial, likelyLimits how much or how often it is true
Quantifiersome, most, approximately, aroundLimits how many, or rounds a number
Impersonal phraseit appears that, it is thought thatTakes you personally out of the claim

Two mistakes to avoid

1. Too strong. "This proves the design is safe" after testing one sample. One counter-example now destroys your whole report. Use suggests or may.
2. Too many hedges. "It could possibly be suggested that there may perhaps be a small effect." Four hedges in one sentence say nothing. One or two is enough.

Quick fixes

Too strongBetter
This proves that vibration causes the failure.This suggests that vibration causes the failure.
The coating always prevents corrosion.The coating generally prevents corrosion.
Everyone knows steel is better.Steel is stronger in most structural uses.
Too weakBetter
It might possibly be a slight tendency.There is a slight tendency.
The results may perhaps confirm the theory.The results confirm the theory.
Note for Spanish speakers: Spanish often uses the future to guess — Será un error del sensor. English does not. Use may be or is probably, not will be.
