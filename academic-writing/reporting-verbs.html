Academic writing

Choosing Your
Citation Verb

Eight verbs, three groups. The verb tells your reader whether you accept a source, doubt it, or plan to argue with it.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

The verb you choose tells your reader how much weight to give a source — before you have written a word of evaluation. Three sentences, identical information, three different positions.

You accept it"Weber (2022) shows that albedo is the main factor."You treat it as proven.
Open to debate"Weber (2022) argues that albedo is the main factor."A position you might contest.
You doubt it"Weber (2022) claims that albedo is the main factor."You signal disagreement — say so only if you mean it.

Eight verbs, three groups

You do not need thirty. These eight cover almost everything you will write.

GroupVerbsUse when
Neutralstates · reports · notesYou are relaying what the source says, no judgement yet
Tentativesuggests · indicatesEvidence points that way but is not conclusive
Committedshows · argues · claimsShows = you accept it · argues = a position · claims = you doubt it
The one to be careful with is claims. It signals doubt. “Newton (1687) claims that force equals mass times acceleration” tells your reader you doubt Newton. Students misuse this verb more than any other.

Grammar

PatternVerbsExample
verb + thatstates, reports, notes, suggests, indicates, shows, argues, claimsPark (2022) suggests that wind speed is decisive.
verb + nounexamines, investigates, describesPark (2022) examines the relationship between…

Never mix them: Park examines that… is ungrammatical.

Tense

  • Present simple for a position that still stands: Park (2022) argues that… This is the default.
  • Past simple for what one study did: Park (2022) measured twelve sites.
  • Present perfect for a body of work: Several studies have examined this.

Errors that come up every year

Instead ofWriteWhy
The author tells that…The author states that…Tell needs a person: tell someone something.
Weber explains about the method.Weber explains the method.No preposition after explain.
Weber talks about albedo.Weber discusses albedo.Talk about is spoken register.
Weber mentions that albedo is decisive.Weber argues that albedo is decisive.Mention means said briefly in passing — it trivialises a central claim.
Weber proves that…Weber shows that…Prove is very strong and rare outside mathematics.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
Nieminen affirms that…Nieminen states / argues that…
Park comments that the method is valid.Park notes that the method is valid.
Silva expresses that the data are limited.Silva acknowledges that the data are limited.
The study realises an analysis of…The study analyses… / carries out an analysis of…
Afirmar is not affirm. English affirm is formal and slightly legal — you affirm an oath. For reporting a source use states, argues or reports.
