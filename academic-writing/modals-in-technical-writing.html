Academic writing · Grammar

Modals: Must,
Should, May

Three jobs, one set of words: obligation, recommendation and possibility. Spanish speakers mix them constantly. Twenty exercises.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Modals do three different jobs in a report, and Spanish speakers routinely pick the wrong one. The difference between must and should is the difference between an order and advice.

Sounds like an order"The supplier must be changed."To a client or supervisor this reads as an instruction from someone with no authority to give one.
Sounds like an engineer"The supplier should be changed."A recommendation, backed by your evidence, that the reader can accept or reject.
Genuine obligation"The guard must be fitted before the machine is started."Must is right here — a safety requirement, not advice.

Three jobs

JobModalsExample
Obligationmust, have to, is required toThe guard must be fitted before operation.
Recommendationshould, could, may want toThe seal should be replaced annually.
Possibilitymay, might, couldFouling may explain the pressure loss.
The trap. Must also expresses deduction — the seal must have failed means "I am almost certain it failed", not "it is required to fail". Same word, two jobs, and context decides.

Where each belongs

  • Safety notices and standards — must, shall, is required to.
  • Conclusions and recommendations — should, could.
  • Discussion — may, might, could for explanations you have not proved.
  • Requests in emails — could you, would it be possible to. Never you must.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglishWhy
The company must improve the design. (La empresa debe…)The company should improve the design.Deber covers both. English must is much stronger than Spanish debe.
It will be a sensor error. (Será un error…)It may be a sensor error.Spanish uses the future to guess. English uses a modal of possibility.
You must send me the datasheet.Could you send me the datasheet?Debe enviarme is neutral in Spanish; you must is an order in English.
We can to improve the efficiency.We can improve the efficiency.No to after a modal.
It must not be possible.It cannot be possible. / That is unlikely.Must not = prohibition, not impossibility.
