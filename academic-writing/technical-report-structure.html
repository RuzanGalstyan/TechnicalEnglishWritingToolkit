Why this matters

Most reports lose marks not for bad English but for putting the right content in the wrong section. Nobody reads a report front to back — each section has to make sense to someone who reads only that section.

Two sections in one sentence"The temperature rose to 78 °C, which shows the insulation had failed."The first half is a result. The second is a discussion. A marker will circle exactly this.
SeparatedResults: "The temperature rose to 78 °C."
Discussion: "This may indicate that the insulation had failed."Report in one place, argue in another — and hedge the argument.

If a sentence does not answer its section's question, it is in the wrong section. That single test catches most structural faults in student reports.

The abstract, move by move

Six moves, always in this order. Learn the sequence and you can write one for any report in twenty minutes — and write it last.

  1. Context — why anyone should care, in one sentence
  2. Aim — exactly what was investigated
  3. Method — what data, over what period, against what comparison
  4. Results — with a number. The most important sentence
  5. Conclusion — interpretation, hedged
  6. Limitation or further work — optional, and it marks a careful writer
No references, no figure numbers, no undefined abbreviations. An abstract must stand alone. And an abstract that says “significant savings were achieved” without saying how much has wasted its most valuable sentence.

Three traps

  • Interpretation in Results. Report there; argue in the Discussion.
  • Re-listing numbers in the Discussion. If the Discussion could be understood without the Results, you have repeated yourself.
  • New material in the Conclusion. Nothing appears there for the first time.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
In this section we will explain the method.This section describes the method. (Or just start.)
The conclusions obtained are the following:The main conclusions are:
In the Figure 3 we can see…Figure 3 shows…
Finally, to conclude, we can conclude that…In conclusion, …
The present work realises an analysis of…This report analyses…
Announcing wastes the sentence. Spanish academic style often signposts what is coming (en este apartado se explicará…). English technical writing prefers to simply do it — headings already tell the reader where they are.
