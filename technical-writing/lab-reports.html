A lab report exists so someone who was not there can judge your work and repeat it. Twenty exercises on sections, numbers and uncertainty.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

A lab report exists so that someone who was not there can judge your work and, if they want to, repeat it. Every convention follows from that one purpose.

Cannot be repeated"We heated the sample for a while until it changed colour, then measured it."How long? What temperature? Measured with what? Nobody can reproduce this, so nobody can check it.
Can be repeated"The sample was heated to 180 °C for 12 minutes, then mass was measured on a balance readable to 0.001 g."Times, temperatures, instrument precision. A stranger could run this tomorrow.

The sections

TitleWhat was investigated?—
AbstractAim, method, main result, conclusion — in 150–200 wordsMixed
IntroductionWhy does this matter, and what is the aim?Present simple
TheoryWhat equations or principles does the reader need?Present simple
MethodWhat exactly did you do?Past simple passive
ResultsWhat happened? (No interpretation)Past simple
DiscussionWhat does it mean, and what went wrong?Present simple, hedged
ConclusionDoes it answer the aim?Present simple
The line students cross most often. Interpretation in Results. "The temperature rose to 78 °C, which shows the insulation failed" is two sentences pretending to be one — the first is a result, the second is a discussion.

Numbers and units

  • Write a zero before a decimal point: 0.7, not .7.
  • English uses a decimal point and a comma for thousands: 1,250.7 — the reverse of Spanish.
  • Do not report more significant figures than your instrument justifies. A balance readable to 0.001 g does not give you 4.10237 g.
  • Give the actual quantity used, not a range: "12.4 mL", not "10–15 mL".
  • Space between number and unit: 180 °C, 25 mm. Exception: 25%.
  • Spell out a number that starts a sentence, or rewrite the sentence.

Uncertainty is not failure

Every measurement has an uncertainty, and reporting it is what makes you credible. So is explaining a result that came out wrong. A discussion that says "the discrepancy is most likely attributable to heat loss through the uninsulated joint" earns more than one claiming everything went perfectly.

Figures and tables

Do not present the same data as both a table and a graph — choose whichever shows the point better. Number every figure, caption it, and refer to it in the text.

Realizar is not realise. Realizar un experimento is to carry out or perform an experiment. English realise means to become aware of something.

Sources

The conventions here are standard across science and engineering laboratories rather than particular to one text. Useful further reading:

  • Last, S. (2019). 7.6 Lab Reports, in Technical Writing Essentials. University of Victoria. CC BY 4.0.
  • Laboratory Report Instructions, Reed College Online Writing Lab — particularly good on the Discussion section and on critiquing your own experimental design.
  • Writing Lab Reports, University of Southampton Library — covers the lab notebook and the writing process as well as the sections.
Check your module handbook first. Laboratory conventions vary between departments — on personal pronouns, on abstract length, on whether a separate Theory section is expected. Where your handbook and this page disagree, follow the handbook.
