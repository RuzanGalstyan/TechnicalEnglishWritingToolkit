Show and tell

A technical description combines words and visuals so a reader can picture something, understand what it does, and grasp how it works. Unlike creative description, it relies mainly on concrete, measurable detail — dimensions, materials, tolerances — rather than impressions.

The stakes are higher than students usually assume. Product descriptions are often legally required for safety and compliance, and a description that misleads can waste weeks or cause an accident. Precision is not a stylistic preference here.

Three kinds

TypeWhat it does
Mechanism descriptionCovers the physical aspects of a tool, machine or device with moving parts that performs a specific function. This tool focuses here.
Process descriptionCovers a sequence of events leading to an outcome — either non-instructional (how an engine works) or instructional (steps to follow).
DefinitionClarifies what a term means in a particular context. Can be a clarifying phrase or several pages.

For the second kind, see Explaining How Something Works, which covers the grammar of process description in detail.

Decide audience and purpose before you write a word. Are you describing solar panels to consumers choosing between them, or supplying schematics to the technician installing them? Same object, almost no shared content. Every later decision — how much detail, which terms need defining, what to leave out — follows from this one.

What the reader must end up with

  • Its general appearance and physical properties
  • Its overall function or purpose
  • Its component parts
  • How those parts interact to make a working whole

The last point is the one weak descriptions miss. A list of components is a parts inventory, not a description — what makes it a description is explaining how they act on each other.

Seven parts

Not every description needs all seven. Judge against your audience: strike a balance between stating the obvious and assuming knowledge your reader may not have.

Part 1

Definition

What is it, and what is it mainly for? One sentence, before anything else.

A hydraulic disc brake is a braking system that converts hand force into friction at the wheel.

Part 2

Overview

The big picture — overall size, shape, general appearance. Give the reader something to hold in mind before you break it apart.

The caliper is roughly 90 mm long and bolts to the frame near the hub.

Part 3

Components

The main parts, in labelled sections. Order matters more than students expect — see the organising principles below.

Part 4

Explanation

How the parts work together, and what principles govern that. This is the part that turns a list into a description.

Part 5

Visuals

Graphics that show the device whole, plus expanded views, cut-aways or labelled diagrams for detail. Every figure numbered, captioned and referred to in the text.

Part 6

Conclusion

Whatever the purpose requires — availability, cost, manufacturing, history, or warnings. Safety warnings are rarely optional.

Part 7

References

Sources used, or further sources worth consulting.

Language

01

Be specific, not general

Vague modifiers do no work. If you can measure it, measure it.

Instead ofWrite
a fairly large rotora 180 mm steel rotor
made of strong materialmachined from 6061 aluminium
gets quite hotreaches up to 250 °C under sustained braking

02

Present tense, active verbs

Describe how the device appears and what it does, as a standing fact. Note the contrast with a method section, which takes the past passive.

The piston presses the pad against the rotor. ✗ The pad was pressed against the rotor by the piston.

03

Objective language — no ad-speak

Subjective praise destroys credibility in a technical document. Describe the property; let the reader draw the conclusion.

Instead ofWrite
outstanding stopping powerreduces stopping distance by 30% in wet conditions
an incredibly reliable designrated for 20,000 hours of operation

04

Correct terminology, defined where needed

Use the right technical term — and define it if your audience may not know it. Which terms need defining is an audience decision, not a vocabulary one.

The rotor is mounted on a six-bolt hub, a fixing standard in which six screws secure the disc to the wheel.

05

Analogy for unfamiliar things

Describe the unfamiliar in terms of the familiar. Used well, one analogy can save a paragraph.

The rocker-bogie suspension works like a see-saw, allowing all six wheels to stay in contact with uneven ground.
A note for Spanish speakers. Spanish tolerates much longer sentences than technical English does. If a sentence runs past about 25 words, look for the second idea inside it and give it its own sentence. Also watch actually (which means currently in Spanish but in fact in English), and the before uncountable and plural generic nouns — steel resists corrosion, not the steel resists corrosion.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
The device is composed by three parts.The device is composed of three parts.
It has a diameter of 40 milimeters.It has a diameter of 40 millimetres.
The piece is made of aluminium.The component / part is made of aluminium.
The scheme shows the assembly.The diagram shows the assembly.
This mechanism permits to regulate the flow.This mechanism regulates the flow.
Two false friends worth memorising. Pieza is a component or part — an English “piece” suggests a fragment. And esquema is a diagram, not a “scheme”.

An annotated description

A short mechanism description written for a general audience — a cycling customer, not a mechanic. Click any highlighted part to see which of the seven parts it performs.

A hydraulic disc brake is a braking system that converts a rider's hand force into friction at the wheel, slowing a bicycle in a controlled manner. The assembly is compact: a caliper roughly 90 mm long bolts to the frame near the wheel hub, straddling a steel rotor between 160 and 203 mm in diameter.

It has four main components: a lever and master cylinder at the handlebar, a sealed hose containing hydraulic fluid, the caliper body housing two opposed pistons, and a pair of replaceable brake pads.

When the lever is squeezed, the master cylinder displaces fluid along the hose. Because the fluid is effectively incompressible, this pressure drives the caliper pistons inwards, pressing the pads against both faces of the rotor. The resulting friction converts the wheel's kinetic energy into heat, which the rotor dissipates into the surrounding air.

Figure 1 shows a cut-away view of the caliper with the pistons and pads labelled. Pads are consumable and should be inspected every few months; pads contaminated with oil lose braking force and cannot be cleaned reliably.
