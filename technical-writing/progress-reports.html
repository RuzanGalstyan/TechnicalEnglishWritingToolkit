Your supervisor needs to know whether to worry. Twenty exercises on reporting what is done, what is late, and what you need.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Your supervisor asks for a progress report because they need to know whether to worry. The report that reassures them is not the one that says everything is fine — it is the one that tells them about the problem while there is still time to fix it.

Stores up trouble"Everything is going well. We are working on the sensor calibration."Two weeks later the project is late and the supervisor finds out with no time to help.
Buys you help"Calibration is two weeks behind because the reference gauge was unavailable. We have booked it for the 12th and can still meet the deadline if testing starts by the 20th."Problem, cause, plan, and what it depends on. Now your supervisor can act.

What goes in one

PartWhat it answers
Purpose and periodWhich project, covering which dates
Work completedWhat is finished since the last report
Work in progressWhat is underway now, and how far along
ProblemsWhat has gone wrong, why, and what you are doing about it
Work plannedWhat comes next, with dates
Overall assessmentOn schedule, or not — stated plainly

Three ways to organise it

  • By time — completed, in progress, planned. The simplest, and usually the best for a short project.
  • By task — one heading per work package. Better when tasks run in parallel and different people own them.
  • By milestone — against the plan you originally agreed. Best when there is a formal schedule to report against.

Reporting a problem well

Four parts, in this order: what happened · why · what you have done · what you need. Leaving out the third makes you look passive; leaving out the fourth leaves your supervisor guessing what you want from them.

Never let a deadline slip silently. A supervisor told early can reallocate equipment, adjust scope or extend a deadline. A supervisor told late can only record that you were late.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglish
We have finished the tests last week.We finished the tests last week.
Actually we are working on the sensors.Currently we are working on the sensors.
We are retarded two weeks.We are two weeks behind schedule.
We expect to finish it the next week.We expect to finish it next week.
We have a delay because of the provider.We are delayed because of the supplier.
Two traps in one sentence. Retrasado is behind schedule or delayed — never "retarded", which in English is offensive. And proveedor is a supplier, not a "provider", in a materials or equipment context.
