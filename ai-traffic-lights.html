AI ethics in writing

Sorting AI Use
into Three Bands

Green, amber, red. Twenty real uses to sort — and the reasoning matters more than the score.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

You will be asked, in a viva or a job interview, why you used a tool the way you did. This framework is not a rulebook — it is a way of answering that question in one sentence.

🟢 GreenNothing generated reaches your submission.Explaining a concept, generating practice questions, unpacking feedback.
🟡 AmberYour work, altered by a tool.Check what your brief permits, then describe what you did.
🔴 RedThe thinking being assessed is not yours.Or someone else's data is involved.

Three bands

BandTestWhat to do
🟢 GreenNothing generated reaches your submissionUse it
🟡 AmberYour work, altered by a toolCheck the brief, then disclose
🔴 RedThe assessed thinking is not yours, or the data is not yoursStop
Amber is not "a bit red". It means the use may well be fine and the decision is not yours alone. Two things: check what your brief permits, and write one sentence describing what you did.

Sorting a use in three questions

AskIf yes
Does anything generated end up in what I submit?Not green
Is the thing it did the thing being assessed?Red
Could I explain and defend every sentence?If no — red, whatever the policy says

The same use, three contexts

ContextBand
Grammar-checking a lab report on a mechanical engineering module🟡 Amber
Grammar-checking an essay on an academic writing module🔴 Red
Grammar-checking a job application letter🟢 Green

The action is identical. What changes is whether written English is the outcome being assessed — which is why the learning outcomes on your brief are the most reliable guide you have.

One line does not move. Confidential data — placement material, personal data, anything under an NDA — is red for legal reasons that apply even where your course permits AI use freely.

Sources

  • Based on Cheng, A., Calhoun, A., & Reedy, G. (2025). Artificial intelligence-assisted academic writing: recommendations for ethical use. Advances in Simulation, 10(1), 22. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41077-025-00350-6 — open access.
This is not policy. The bands here reflect what is typical across many institutions in 2026. Your assignment brief and your institution's current guidance override anything on this page, and both change quickly. If a use sits in amber and you cannot find guidance, ask your teacher.
Scope of the source. Cheng et al. wrote for researchers preparing journal manuscripts, not for students on taught programmes. The principles transfer; the context does not always.
