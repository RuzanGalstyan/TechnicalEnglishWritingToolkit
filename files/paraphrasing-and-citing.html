Using Sources
Without Copying

Why swapping synonyms is still plagiarism, and a method that avoids it. Twenty exercises.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Changing the words but keeping the sentence is called patchwriting, and it counts as plagiarism. Adding a citation does not fix it — a citation attributes an idea, it does not license reuse of the sentence that carried it.

PatchwritingOriginal: "Urban surfaces such as asphalt absorb solar radiation during the day."
Yours: "Urban surfaces like asphalt take in solar radiation during the daytime (Nieminen, 2023)."Same structure, same order, synonyms swapped. The citation does not rescue it.
A real paraphrase"Nieminen (2023) attributes the temperature gap to the thermal behaviour of built surfaces: asphalt stores daytime solar energy and releases it overnight."Reordered, regrammared, and integrated into your own sentence.

Five steps that work

The critical step is the second. If the original is visible while you write, you will patchwrite — everyone does.

  1. Read until you could explain it to a classmate.
  2. Cover it. Close the tab, turn the page over.
  3. Write the idea from memory, in a sentence that fits your argument.
  4. Compare with the original — check accuracy, and check you have not drifted back into their phrasing.
  5. Add the citation.

What has to change

ChangeHow
Sentence orderPut the result first and the cause second, or the reverse
GrammarActive ↔ passive; verb ↔ noun
Clause structureSplit one long sentence into two, or combine two
FocusStart from the part that matters to your argument
VocabularyLast, and least important. Technical terms stay as they are
Do not invent synonyms for technical terms. Urban heat island effect and thermocouple have no acceptable alternatives — replacing them makes your writing worse and harder to search.

Quote, paraphrase or summary?

TypeWhat it isNeeds
QuotationThe original wordsQuotation marks + citation + page number
ParaphraseOne passage in your words, similar lengthCitation
SummaryA longer passage compressedCitation

Quote rarely. Only when the exact wording is the point — a legal definition, a technical standard, a phrase you will analyse. If a third of your report is quotations, something has gone wrong.

Two ways to place a citation

Author-prominent: Nieminen (2023) argues that surface materials dominate. Use when the source or the disagreement is part of your point.

Information-prominent: Surface materials dominate (Nieminen, 2023). Use when the fact matters more than who found it, or when several sources agree.

Alternating between them is what stops a literature review reading as a list.

Following Park, the method is valid.According to Park, the method is valid.

Double attribution. Según Nieminen, él afirma que… is acceptable in Spanish. In English, According to X and X states are two frames doing one job — choose one.
