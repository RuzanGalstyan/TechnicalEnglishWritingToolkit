Verb Tenses by Section

You do not need the whole English tense system. You need four choices, each tied to a place in your report. Twenty exercises.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

Markers spot a wrong tense instantly, because in a report the tense tells the reader what kind of statement they are reading — a known fact, something you did, something you found, or something you think it means.

Wrong"The samples are heated to 180 °C and the mass loss was 4%."Present for your own procedure, past for the result. The reader cannot tell whether this is how it is always done or what you did.
Right"The samples were heated to 180 °C. The mass loss was 4%."Past passive for your method, past simple for your result. Two clear statements.

Four choices

SectionTenseExample
IntroductionPresent simpleEnergy demand is rising in the sector.
IntroductionPresent perfectSeveral studies have examined this effect.
TheoryPresent simpleHeat transfer occurs by three mechanisms.
MethodPast simple passiveThe sensor was calibrated before each run.
ResultsPast simpleOutput decreased by 12%.
ResultsPresent simple (figures)Figure 3 shows the relationship.
DiscussionPresent simple, hedgedThis suggests that fouling is the main cause.
ConclusionPresent simple / modalThe method is suitable. Further testing should be carried out.
The one rule underneath. Past for what happened once — your procedure, your results. Present for what is still true — established knowledge, what a figure shows, what you think your results mean.

Two tricky cases

Figures live in the present. Your experiment finished months ago, but Figure 3 shows… is present, because the figure still shows it now.

Your own results in the Discussion. Refer back in the past — output decreased by 12% — but interpret in the present — this suggests fouling.

For Spanish speakers

From SpanishEnglishWhy
I have finished the test yesterday.I finished the test yesterday.English never uses the present perfect with a finished past time.
I am working here since March.I have been working here since March.Spanish uses the present with desde; English needs the perfect.
The results are showing a clear trend.The results show a clear trend.Están mostrando is normal Spanish; English prefers the simple present for facts.
In this report I explain the method.This report explains the method.Fine grammar, but reports avoid I in many programmes — check your brief.
