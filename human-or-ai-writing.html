Twelve passages to judge, then eight items on why style is not evidence of authorship. Twenty exercises.

Designed by Ruzana Galstyan-Sargsyan · Universitat Politècnica de València

Why this matters

This is not a detection exercise. The useful skill is recognising empty fluent prose — writing that is grammatically flawless, well organised, and says almost nothing. That failing predates chatbots and is the commonest weakness in student reports.

Says nothing"Renewable energy plays a crucial role in the modern energy landscape, offering numerous benefits including reduced emissions and improved security."Nothing here could be wrong, because nothing is specific enough to be wrong.
Says something"We fitted the new inverter in March because the old one kept tripping on cold mornings. Output is up about 8%, though some of that is a better spring."A date, a symptom, a number — and the writer undercutting their own figure.

Two registers

Often signals empty proseOften signals lived experience
Even sentence lengths, smooth rhythmUneven rhythm, some very short sentences
Lists of three, neatly parallelAwkward specifics that resist parallelism
Abstract nouns, no numbersDates, model numbers, place names
Confident but unfalsifiable claimsHedges, doubts, things that went wrong
"Plays a crucial role", "it is important to note"Domain shorthand, mild irritation, asides
The test that matters. After reading a paragraph, ask: what did I learn that I could not have guessed? If the answer is nothing, the writing has failed — whoever or whatever produced it.

What this cannot tell you

Studies of AI-detection tools have repeatedly found high error rates in both directions, and light editing usually defeats them. Several universities have withdrawn or disabled detection tools for that reason.

The fairness problem, which matters most here. Detection tools and human graders both flag second-language writing disproportionately. Careful non-native academic English tends to use conventional phrasing, avoid idiom and keep structures regular — the very features people read as “machine-like”. If you study in English as an additional language, this risk falls on you, not on native speakers.

So never accuse anyone, including yourself, on the basis of style. Where authorship is genuinely in question, the meaningful evidence is process: drafts, version history, notes, and whether the writer can discuss their own work.

What it means for your own writing

  • Specificity is both your quality and your protection. Numbers, dates, equipment, what went wrong.
  • Keep your drafts. Version history in Word or Docs answers any question about how a text came about.
  • Be able to explain every sentence. If you cannot say why a claim is there, it should not be there.
